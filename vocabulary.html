Academic vocabulary

100 words · meanings · example sentences

100 words10 minutes a day

100 useful academic words for reading and writing. Learn ten a day and write your own example sentence for each.

  1. abundant: existing in large quantities. Fresh water is abundant in the north of the country.
  2. accelerate: to make something happen faster. Online learning accelerated during the pandemic.
  3. accumulate: to gather or build up over time. Dust accumulates quickly in a dry climate.
  4. accurate: correct in every detail. The survey gave an accurate picture of student habits.
  5. acknowledge: to accept that something is true. The report acknowledges the limits of its data.
  6. adequate: enough for a purpose. Many villages still lack adequate health care.
  7. advocate: to publicly support an idea. Doctors advocate regular exercise for all ages.
  8. allocate: to give a share of something for a purpose. The city allocated more money to public parks.
  9. alter: to change. Climate change may alter the routes of migrating birds.
  10. ambiguous: having more than one possible meaning. The instructions were ambiguous, so students were confused.
  11. analyse: to examine something in detail. Researchers analysed data from 2,000 households.
  12. anticipate: to expect something to happen. Planners anticipate a rise in demand for housing.
  13. apparent: easy to see or understand. It soon became apparent that the plan was too expensive.
  14. approximately: close to but not exactly. The building is approximately 200 years old.
  15. arbitrary: based on chance rather than reason. The age limit seemed arbitrary to many parents.
  16. assess: to judge the quality or value of something. Teachers assess students' progress every term.
  17. assumption: something believed without proof. The study challenges the assumption that boys prefer science.
  18. attribute: to say something is caused by something else. The fall in crime was attributed to better street lighting.
  19. beneficial: having a good effect. A balanced diet is beneficial to health.
  20. bias: an unfair preference for or against something. Researchers must avoid bias when choosing participants.
  21. capacity: the amount something can hold or produce. The stadium has a capacity of 60,000.
  22. coherent: logical and clearly connected. A coherent essay moves smoothly from one idea to the next.
  23. coincide: to happen at the same time. The festival coincides with the school holidays.
  24. collapse: to fall down or fail suddenly. Several fish populations collapsed due to overfishing.
  25. compatible: able to exist or work together. The new software is not compatible with older computers.
  26. compensate: to make up for a loss. Farmers were compensated for damaged crops.
  27. comprehensive: including everything. The guide offers a comprehensive list of museums.
  28. comprise: to consist of. The committee comprises ten members.
  29. conceive: to form an idea. The project was conceived by two students.
  30. consecutive: following one after another. Prices rose for five consecutive months.
  31. consequence: a result of an action. One consequence of deforestation is soil erosion.
  32. considerable: large in size or amount. The bridge required a considerable investment.
  33. constrain: to limit. A small budget constrained the research team.
  34. contemporary: belonging to the present time. The gallery shows contemporary art.
  35. contradict: to state the opposite. The new findings contradict earlier studies.
  36. contribute: to help cause something. Many factors contribute to traffic congestion.
  37. controversial: causing strong disagreement. The plan to close the library was controversial.
  38. crucial: extremely important. Sleep is crucial for memory.
  39. decline: to become less. The number of bank branches has declined sharply.
  40. deduce: to reach a conclusion by reasoning. From the evidence, we can deduce that the site was a market.
  41. demonstrate: to show clearly. The experiment demonstrated that plants respond to sound.
  42. derive: to obtain from a source. Many English words derive from Latin.
  43. deteriorate: to become worse. Air quality deteriorates in winter.
  44. diminish: to reduce. The importance of print newspapers has diminished.
  45. discrepancy: a difference between things that should match. There was a discrepancy between the two sets of figures.
  46. distinct: clearly different. The region has a distinct culture.
  47. diverse: very varied. London has a diverse population.
  48. dominant: most powerful or important. Tourism is the dominant industry on the island.
  49. eliminate: to remove completely. Vaccination has nearly eliminated some diseases.
  50. emerge: to appear or become known. New evidence emerged after the report was published.
  51. emphasis: special importance. The course places emphasis on practical skills.
  52. empirical: based on observation or experiment. The theory lacks empirical support.
  53. enhance: to improve. Good lighting can enhance a room.
  54. equivalent: equal in value or meaning. One mile is equivalent to about 1.6 kilometres.
  55. estimate: to make an approximate calculation. Experts estimate that the population will double.
  56. evaluate: to judge the value of something. The study evaluates three teaching methods.
  57. evident: clear and obvious. Her talent was evident from an early age.
  58. exceed: to be greater than. Demand exceeded supply last summer.
  59. expand: to become larger. The company expanded into Asia.
  60. exploit: to use something fully, sometimes unfairly. The region exploits its solar energy potential.
  61. facilitate: to make easier. Clear signs facilitate movement through the airport.
  62. feasible: possible and practical. A high-speed rail link is technically feasible.
  63. fluctuate: to change level often. Fuel prices fluctuate throughout the year.
  64. fundamental: basic and essential. Reading is a fundamental skill.
  65. generate: to produce. Wind farms generate electricity.
  66. hypothesis: an idea to be tested. The hypothesis was supported by the results.
  67. identical: exactly the same. The twins wore identical jackets.
  68. implement: to put a plan into action. The school implemented a new timetable.
  69. implication: a possible effect. The findings have implications for public health.
  70. incentive: something that encourages action. Tax incentives encourage companies to recycle.
  71. inevitable: certain to happen. Some change is inevitable as cities grow.
  72. infrastructure: basic systems such as roads and power. Rural infrastructure needs investment.
  73. inherent: existing as a natural part. There are risks inherent in any new technology.
  74. initiate: to start. The government initiated a recycling campaign.
  75. innovation: a new idea or method. Innovation drives economic growth.
  76. integrate: to combine into a whole. Schools integrate technology into lessons.
  77. interpret: to explain the meaning of. Students must interpret data from graphs.
  78. justify: to give good reasons for. Can the high cost be justified?
  79. maintain: to keep in good condition or at the same level. Older buildings are expensive to maintain.
  80. marginal: very small. There was only a marginal increase in sales.
  81. mechanism: a system or process. The body has mechanisms to control temperature.
  82. minimise: to reduce to the smallest amount. Good planning minimises waste.
  83. modify: to change slightly. The design was modified after testing.
  84. monitor: to watch and check over time. Scientists monitor sea levels.
  85. notion: an idea or belief. The notion that money brings happiness is debated.
  86. obtain: to get. Participants obtained permission from their parents.
  87. outcome: a result. The outcome of the experiment surprised everyone.
  88. perceive: to notice or understand in a certain way. Many people perceive cities as unsafe at night.
  89. phenomenon: an event or fact that can be observed. Migration is a global phenomenon.
  90. predominantly: mainly. The workforce is predominantly young.
  91. preliminary: coming before the main part. Preliminary results look promising.
  92. prospect: a possibility of something in the future. There is little prospect of rain this week.
  93. rational: based on reason. We need a rational discussion of the issue.
  94. relevant: connected to the subject. Only include relevant information.
  95. reluctant: unwilling. Some owners are reluctant to invest in green roofs.
  96. significant: large or important. There was a significant rise in temperature.
  97. substantial: large in amount. The project received substantial funding.
  98. sustainable: able to continue without harming the environment. Sustainable fishing protects future stocks.
  99. trend: a general direction of change. There is a trend towards smaller families.
  100. undermine: to weaken gradually. Rumours can undermine trust in science.
