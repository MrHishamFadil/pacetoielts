Question-type lessons

Know each task before you practise it

Short, practical lessons on each question type. Read one, then practise straight away.

True / False / Not Given

Decide whether a statement agrees with the facts in the passage.

  1. FALSE means the passage says the opposite.
  2. NOT GIVEN means the passage says nothing either way; do not use your own knowledge.
  3. Watch for words like all, always and only: they often make a statement false.

Matching headings

Choose the heading that sums up the main idea of each paragraph.

  1. Read the first and last sentences of the paragraph first.
  2. Ignore headings that only match one detail.
  3. There are more headings than paragraphs.

Sentence and summary completion

Fill gaps with words from the passage.

  1. Check the word limit, e.g. NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS.
  2. Predict the type of word (noun, number, adjective) before you look.
  3. Copy spelling exactly from the text.

Multiple choice

Choose the best option from A–D.

  1. Find the part of the text first, then compare each option.
  2. Wrong options often use words from the passage with a changed meaning.
  3. Choose the option that answers the exact question.

Form and note completion (Listening)

Write down names, numbers and details you hear.

  1. Spelled names and numbers are common; practise the alphabet.
  2. Speakers often change their minds: listen for the final answer.
  3. Plurals matter: tyre and tyres are different answers.

Writing Task 1 overview

Summarise the most important trends in 150+ words.

  1. Write an overview sentence with the two or three key features.
  2. Group data; do not list every number.
  3. No opinions or causes in Task 1.

Writing Task 2 essay types

Opinion, discussion, advantages/disadvantages, problem/solution.

  1. Identify the type from the question words.
  2. Answer every part of the question.
  3. One main idea per paragraph, with a clear example.

Speaking fluency

Talk naturally and extend your answers.

  1. Answer, give a reason, add an example.
  2. Use the 1 minute in Part 2 to write keywords.
  3. It is fine to correct yourself; keep going.
