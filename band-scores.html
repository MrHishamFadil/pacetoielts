Band score guide

What examiners look for, explained simply

This is our own plain-English guide to how speaking and writing are judged. For the official descriptors, see the IELTS website.

Bands are summarised by us for learning purposes only.

Task response / Task achievement

Do you answer the whole question, with a clear position and well-developed ideas?

Around band 5Around band 7Around band 9
Ideas are only partly relevant or very general.All parts are answered; main ideas are extended and supported.A fully developed answer with a clear, consistent position.

Coherence and cohesion

Is your answer logically organised and easy to follow?

Around band 5Around band 7Around band 9
Paragraphing is unclear; linking words are repetitive.Clear progression; a range of linking devices used well.Ideas flow naturally; linking is almost invisible.

Lexical resource (vocabulary)

How wide and accurate is your vocabulary?

Around band 5Around band 7Around band 9
Limited range; errors sometimes cause confusion.A good range, some less common words, occasional errors.Wide, precise, natural vocabulary; very rare slips.

Grammatical range and accuracy

Can you use varied structures correctly?

Around band 5Around band 7Around band 9
Mostly simple sentences; frequent errors.A mix of complex structures; many error-free sentences.Wide range used flexibly; errors are rare.

Speaking: fluency and pronunciation

Can you speak at length, clearly and naturally?

Around band 5Around band 7Around band 9
Frequent pauses; some words hard to understand.Speaks at length with some hesitation; easy to understand.Fluent, natural rhythm and intonation throughout.

Reading and Listening

These are marked by the number of correct answers out of 40, then converted to a band. The exact conversion varies slightly between test versions, so use your practice scores as a guide to progress, not as a prediction.
