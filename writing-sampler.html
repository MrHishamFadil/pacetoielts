A 5-minute Writing sample

No sign-in needed. Practise one IELTS Academic Writing Task 2 body paragraph.

In Academic Writing Task 2 you discuss a point of view, argument or problem. You should spend about 40 minutes on this task and write at least 250 words. Task 2 contributes twice as much as Task 1 to the Writing score.

This short practice does not give a band score, and it does not give instant tutor feedback. You will choose a fix, then see a worked explanation and an improved paragraph.

The task

An original practice prompt, not an official IELTS question

Some towns are moving their weekly open-air produce markets into large indoor food halls. Some people say this is more convenient and more hygienic. Others believe the open-air market is better for local growers and for the character of the town.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

A weak body paragraph

Indoor food halls are clearly better for local growers. Everyone knows that sales increase a lot when a market moves inside, and the atmosphere is more professional. This is good for the town and it also helps customers. For example, many places have seen great results after making this change, so councils should do the same as soon as possible.

Which change would do most to fix this paragraph?

Why this matters

The paragraph states a clear point — that indoor halls help local growers — but it does not support that point. Phrases such as “everyone knows”, “sales increase a lot” and “many places have seen great results” are claims without evidence. Official Task 2 guidance asks you to develop your argument and to use relevant examples or evidence, which may come from your own experience.

Adding formal linking words would not fix the missing support. Shortening the paragraph to save time for Task 1 is unwise, because Task 2 carries twice the weight of Task 1, and the essay as a whole needs at least 250 words. Changing the topic would make the paragraph irrelevant, and answers that drift off the task are penalised.

An improved version

Moving a weekly market into an indoor food hall can help local growers in one practical way: they can still trade in wet or very hot weather, when an open square would otherwise stay empty. For instance, a fruit grower who now sets up under a permanent roof can keep regular Saturday hours through the rainy season, so familiar customers still come and less stock is left to spoil. This does not automatically raise every stall’s income, but it does remove a weather-related interruption that an open-air site cannot avoid.

A quicker follow-up

The improved paragraph still needs a closing sentence. Which ending is better?

Choose the best closing sentence

The first ending overgeneralises: the paragraph only supported a weather-related benefit, not a rule for every town. The third ending invents a study and a statistic. Do not invent research or figures in Task 2. Official guidance allows examples from your own experience; it does not ask you to invent studies. The second ending closes the point at the same strength as the evidence.
