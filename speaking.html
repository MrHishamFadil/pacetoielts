Speaking practice

30 Part 1 questions · 15 cue cards · 30 Part 3 questions · model answers

How to practise

Record yourself on your phone, then listen back. Use a 1-minute timer to prepare each cue card and talk for up to 2 minutes.

Practise aloud and record yourself. Extend answers with reasons and examples.

Part 1 (30 questions)

Part 1 of 34–5 minutes

Home and accommodation

  1. Do you live in a house or a flat?
  2. What is your favourite room in your home? Why?
  3. Would you like to move to a different home in the future?
  4. Who do you live with?
  5. What would you change about where you live?

Work or study

  1. Do you work or are you a student?
  2. Why did you choose that subject or job?
  3. What do you enjoy most about it?
  4. Is there anything difficult about it?
  5. What would you like to do in the future?

Daily routine

  1. What time do you usually get up?
  2. What is the busiest part of your day?
  3. Do you prefer mornings or evenings?
  4. Has your routine changed in recent years?
  5. What do you do to relax after a long day?

Food

  1. What kind of food do you like?
  2. Do you enjoy cooking?
  3. Do you prefer eating at home or in restaurants?
  4. Is there any food you disliked as a child?
  5. What is a traditional dish in your country?

Weather and seasons

  1. What is the weather like where you live?
  2. Which season do you like best?
  3. Does the weather affect your mood?
  4. Do you check the weather forecast?
  5. What do people in your area do in hot weather?

Technology

  1. How often do you use your phone?
  2. Which app do you use most?
  3. Do you think you spend too much time online?
  4. Did you use computers at school?
  5. What new technology would you like to buy?

Part 2: Cue cards (15)

Part 2 of 33–4 minutes

Listen to model answers

Three British-English model answers for Cards 1, 2 and 4.

Model answer 1

Read model answer 1: Describe a book that you enjoyed reading.

I'd like to talk about a novel called The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter, which I read last summer. A friend lent it to me before a long train journey, and to be honest I didn't expect much. The story follows a young woman who looks after a lighthouse on a remote island after her father falls ill. What I enjoyed most was the way the writer described the sea; I could almost hear the waves. There's also a lot about courage and responsibility, because she has to make difficult decisions on her own. I finished it in two days, which is unusual for me. The reason it was memorable is that it made me think about how much we rely on other people, and how strong we can be when we have no choice. Since then, I've recommended it to several friends, and I'd happily read it again.

Model answer 2

Read model answer 2: Describe a place you visited that was very peaceful.

The most peaceful place I've visited is a small valley in the mountains, about two hours from my city. I went there with my cousins a couple of years ago, early in spring. We left the car at the end of a narrow road and walked for around an hour along a stream. When we reached the top, there was absolutely no traffic noise, just birds and running water. We sat on the rocks, had a simple picnic, and nobody even looked at their phone, partly because there was no signal. I think it was special because my everyday life is quite busy and loud, so the silence felt almost strange at first. Afterwards, I felt calm for days. Whenever I feel stressed now, I try to remember that afternoon, and I'm planning to go back with my parents.

Model answer 3

Read model answer 3: Describe a skill you would like to learn.

A skill I'd really like to learn is photography, and I mean properly, not just taking pictures on my phone. I've always admired people who can make an ordinary street look beautiful. I'd probably start with an online course to understand the basics, such as light, composition and how to use manual settings. Then I'd practise every weekend, perhaps in the old part of my city, where the buildings have a lot of character. The main reason I want to learn it is that I'd like to record the places and people around me before they change, because my neighbourhood is developing very quickly. I also think it would make me more observant in general. The difficult part will be finding the time, but I believe even thirty minutes a week would make a difference.

You have one minute to prepare and should speak for one to two minutes.

Part 3: Discussion (30 questions)

Part 3 of 34–5 minutes

Reading and learning

  1. Do people read less today than in the past?
  2. Should schools teach children to read for pleasure?
  3. How has the internet changed the way people learn?
  4. Will printed books disappear?
  5. Is it better to learn alone or in a group?

Places and cities

  1. What makes a city a good place to live?
  2. Why do many people move from the countryside to cities?
  3. Should old buildings be protected?
  4. How can cities reduce noise and pollution?
  5. Will cities be very different in 50 years?

People and relationships

  1. What qualities make a good teacher?
  2. Is it important to help strangers?
  3. How has family life changed in your country?
  4. Do young people respect older people today?
  5. Why do some people prefer to work alone?

Skills and work

  1. Which skills will be most important in the future?
  2. Should practical skills be taught at school?
  3. Is it better to have one career or several?
  4. How does technology change the workplace?
  5. Why do some people find it hard to set goals?

Leisure and celebrations

  1. Why are traditional festivals important?
  2. Have celebrations become too commercial?
  3. Should governments invest in sports facilities?
  4. Do people have more free time than before?
  5. Is watching sport as beneficial as playing it?

Technology and society

  1. Do apps make life easier or more complicated?
  2. Should children's screen time be limited?
  3. How can people protect their privacy online?
  4. Will artificial intelligence change education?
  5. Is it possible to live without a smartphone today?
Show answer

Answer frame for Part 3: give your view, explain a reason, add an example, then consider another side. For example: "I think people read less printed material, mainly because phones are always with us. My younger brother reads news only on apps. That said, audiobooks mean some people actually consume more books than before."
