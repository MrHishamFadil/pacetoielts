Writing practice

6 Task 1 data sets · 15 Task 2 prompts · model outlines

Before you write

Check the band score guide to see what examiners look for: task response, coherence, vocabulary and grammar. Write on paper or in a document, then compare with the model outline.

Task 1: write at least 150 words in about 20 minutes. Task 2: write at least 250 words in about 40 minutes. The model outlines show one good way to organise an answer.

Task 1: describing data

6 tasks20 minutes each150+ words

Task 1.1 (Line graph)

The table shows the percentage of households with a home internet connection in three countries, 2004–2024. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

YearCountry ACountry BCountry C
200435%12%58%
200952%20%71%
201468%37%82%
201979%61%90%
202486%78%93%
Show answer

Model outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the task in one sentence.
  2. Overview: all three countries rose; Country C stayed highest, Country B grew fastest and narrowed the gap.
  3. Body 1: Country C from 58% to 93%, slowing after 2014; Country A from 35% to 86%.
  4. Body 2: Country B started lowest at 12%, then rose sharply after 2014, reaching 78% by 2024.

Tip: Group countries with similar patterns; do not describe every number.

Task 1.2 (Bar chart)

The table shows average weekly hours spent on four leisure activities by people aged 16–24 and 65+ in one country in 2023. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

ActivityAge 16–24Age 65+
Online video144
Television519
Sport and exercise63
Reading books27
Show answer

Model outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the task in one sentence.
  2. Overview: young people spent most time on online video, older people on television; the groups had opposite preferences.
  3. Body 1: screen time: video 14 vs 4 hours; television 5 vs 19 hours.
  4. Body 2: sport higher among the young (6 vs 3); reading higher among the old (7 vs 2).

Tip: Use comparatives: 'almost four times as many hours as'.

Task 1.3 (Pie charts)

The table shows how a city's household waste was treated in 2010 and 2025. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Method20102025
Landfill62%24%
Recycling18%41%
Composting8%20%
Energy recovery12%15%
Show answer

Model outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the task in one sentence.
  2. Overview: landfill fell sharply while recycling and composting grew; recycling became the main method.
  3. Body 1: landfill dropped from 62% to 24%.
  4. Body 2: recycling more than doubled to 41%; composting rose from 8% to 20%; energy recovery changed only slightly.

Tip: With percentages, use 'accounted for', 'a share of', 'a quarter'.

Task 1.4 (Process)

The stages below describe how recycled glass bottles become new bottles. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

StageDescription
1Bottles collected from homes and bottle banks
2Sorted by colour at a recycling plant
3Washed to remove labels and dirt
4Crushed into small pieces called cullet
5Cullet mixed with sand and heated to 1,500°C
6Molten glass shaped in moulds
7New bottles cooled, checked and sent to factories
Show answer

Model outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the task in one sentence.
  2. Overview: seven stages, from collection to delivery; the process is cyclical.
  3. Body 1: stages 1–4 (collection, sorting, washing, crushing).
  4. Body 2: stages 5–7 (melting, moulding, cooling and distribution).

Tip: Use the passive and sequencers: 'Once the bottles have been sorted, they are washed'.

Task 1.5 (Table)

The table shows the number of international students (thousands) at universities in four countries. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Country201520202025
W12095140
X6088130
Y210230225
Z454038
Show answer

Model outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the task in one sentence.
  2. Overview: Y had the most students throughout; X grew steadily, Z declined slightly.
  3. Body 1: Y stable around 210–230; W dipped to 95 in 2020 before recovering to 140.
  4. Body 2: X more than doubled from 60 to 130; Z fell gradually from 45 to 38.

Tip: Highlight the highest, the lowest and any change of direction.

Task 1.6 (Map)

The table compares a coastal village in 2000 and today. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Feature2000Today
North of the main roadFarmlandHousing estate and school
Beach areaFishing hutsHotel and car park
Village centreSmall shopSupermarket and cafés
East sideForestForest (smaller), cycle path added
TransportOne bus routeBus station and ferry pier
Show answer

Model outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the task in one sentence.
  2. Overview: the village changed from a farming and fishing community into a tourist and residential area.
  3. Body 1: north and centre: farmland replaced by housing and a school; shop replaced by a supermarket.
  4. Body 2: coast and east: hotel and car park on the beach; forest reduced; transport links expanded.

Tip: Use location phrases: 'to the north of', 'was replaced by', 'was converted into'.

Task 2: essays

15 prompts40 minutes each250+ words

Task 2.1

Some people believe university education should be free for all students. Others think students should pay. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Show answer

Model band-7-style outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase the question and state your opinion.
  2. Body 1: first view with a reason and an example.
  3. Body 2: second view with a reason and an example.
  4. Conclusion: summarise both sides and restate your opinion.

Tip: Give each view its own paragraph and make your own opinion clear.

Task 2.2

In many cities, people now work from home. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?

Show answer

Model band-7-style outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase and say whether advantages or disadvantages are greater.
  2. Body 1: two main advantages, each with an example.
  3. Body 2: two main disadvantages, explaining why they matter less (or more).
  4. Conclusion: a clear final judgement.

Tip: Answer the exact question: say which side is greater.

Task 2.3

Governments should spend more on public transport than on building new roads. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Show answer

Model band-7-style outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase and state your position clearly (agree, disagree or partly agree).
  2. Body 1: main reason for your position, with an example.
  3. Body 2: second reason, or acknowledge the opposite view and explain why it is weaker.
  4. Conclusion: restate your position in new words.

Tip: Keep the same position from introduction to conclusion.

Task 2.4

Many young people today know more about international celebrities than about their own country's history. Why is this? What can be done?

Show answer

Model band-7-style outline:

  1. Introduction: paraphrase and say there are several causes and practical solutions.
  2. Body 1: two main causes, each explained.
  3. Body 2: two matching solutions, saying who should act.
  4. Conclusion: summarise the causes and solutions.

Tip: Link each solution to a cause.

Task 2.8

Show answer

  1. Introduction: paraphrase and mention that the trend creates problems that can be managed.
  2. Body 1: two problems (for example, healthcare costs, pressure on pensions).
  3. Body 2: a solution for each problem.
  4. Conclusion: summarise.

Tip: Make solutions specific and realistic.
